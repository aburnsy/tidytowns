## Description

Tipperary County Council does not directly maintain the village's hard surfaces and verges day to day. The Tidy Towns committee receives a council grant which we use to fund a local contractor for the village ground-maintenance round, and the contractor's standard practice includes chemical weed treatment on hard surfaces. We want to be honest about that. The committee's own work in the beds and planters has shifted toward hand-weeding and timing, but the contractor-delivered round is still chemical-based and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

Our ambition is for the village to have moved away from chemical weed management by **2031**. That is a deliberately gradual horizon. We are conscious that the contractors we rely on, and a fair number of villagers, are used to the way it has been done for years; abruptly banning chemicals would put people off Tidy Towns rather than bring them with us. The transition has to be planned, communicated, and funded properly.

## What we are actually committing to in 2026

- **Document the current position honestly** in our application and on this site. Council does not maintain the village day to day; we fund a contractor via a council grant; the contractor uses chemicals. Saying so is the start of changing it.
- **Research alternatives** that are both cost-effective and time-effective at the scale of the village's hard surfaces and edges. Hot-water weeding, mechanical brushing, vinegar-based treatments, edge-redesign, and changes to mowing height all sit on the candidate list.
- **Open a conversation with our contractor** about what would and wouldn't work on his round, and what the price impact would be.
- **Open a conversation with the council** about whether the grant we receive can support a higher-cost non-chemical alternative.

## What we are not doing in 2026

- Banning chemical use overnight.
- Promising the contractor a switch we haven't paid for or scoped.
- Claiming on the public site that we have already moved away from chemical herbicides; we haven't, and the application narrative says so.

## Best practice we will look at

The [All-Ireland Pollinator Plan grassland and hard-surface guidance](https://pollinators.ie/grasslands/) is the natural reference. Other Tidy Towns committees who have already made this transition will be a useful informal source as the conversation develops, on the same village-to-village neighbour basis that the rest of our work runs on.

## What success looks like by 2031

- A village ground-maintenance round delivered without routine herbicide use.
- A contractor and a council relationship that have been brought along with the change rather than blindsided by it.
- A practice the next 5-year plan can hand on as established rather than aspirational.
