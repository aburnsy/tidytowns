## Description

Phase 1 ([project 028](../028-bog-walk-nature-trail/index.md)) reopens the loop. Phase 2 ([project 029](../029-bog-walk-surface-upgrade/index.md)) makes it accessible. Phase 3 turns it into an actual nature trail by adding biodiversity interpretation, so a walk on the loop teaches people what's growing and living around them.

Slated for 2029. It only makes sense after Phases 1 and 2 are in the ground and the surface is settled enough to attach panels to it.

## What Phase 3 covers

Two staged pieces:

- **Interpretation panels and species ID boards along the bog walk route.** Habitat panels for the bog itself, species ID boards for the most identifiable plants and animals along the loop, a small "what to look for in this season" element. The biodiversity counterpart to the heritage panels at the entrance.
- **Wildlife information panels at 3 to 4 additional key spots around the village** once the trail panels are in. Pollinator corner at the sensory garden, the Castlepark sycamore corner as a wildflower-from-seed-bank story, the church grounds, and one estate's wildflower patch are obvious candidates.

## How this sits with the other interpretation projects

- [Heritage interpretation panels (020)](../../future/020-heritage-interpretation-panels/index.md) covers the heritage story at the castle, forge, church and the bog walk **entrance**. Phase 3 is the biodiversity story **along** the trail, so the two are designed to sit beside each other rather than overlap. The bog walk entrance is the natural place for a heritage panel and a biodiversity panel side by side.
- [Heritage trail (025)](../../future/025-heritage-trail/index.md) is the wayfinding layer pulling castle, forge, church and bog walk together. Phase 3 panels would be the biodiversity content the trail walks visitors past.

## Best practice & research

- [All-Ireland Pollinator Plan](https://pollinators.ie/), guidance on interpretation that helps visitors identify and value native species and habitats.
- [Leave No Trace Ireland](https://www.leavenotraceireland.org/), interpretation should encourage protection of the habitat, not concentrate footfall in fragile spots.
