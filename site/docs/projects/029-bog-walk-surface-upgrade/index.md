## Description

Phase 1 ([project 028](../028-bog-walk-nature-trail/index.md)) clears the route and lays a stone base course so the loop is usable again. Phase 2 takes the next step: a proper surface treatment so the loop is accessible to buggies, kids' bikes and walkers who aren't sure-footed enough for a rough track.

A finished, accessible loop is what turns the Bog Walk from a recovered amenity into a genuinely inclusive village asset, and gives us a credible "Leave No Trace" amenity to point to in the application.

## What Phase 2 covers

- Surface treatment along the loop suited to buggies and bikes, scoped after Phase 1 finishes so we know what the base course actually needs on top of it
- Any drainage tweaks the surface upgrade exposes
- A short access review at the entry points so the surface upgrade isn't undone by an awkward step or kerb at the start

## Funding

Once Phase 1's LEADER money is recouped it rolls into this phase as the seed fund. The committee will apply for a second LEADER round in 2027 to cover the surfacing itself.

## What comes after

[**Phase 3 (2029)**](../030-bog-walk-biodiversity-interpretation/index.md): biodiversity interpretation panels along the trail, then wildlife panels at a handful of village spots. Held off until 2029 so the panels go in once the surface has settled.

## Best practice & research

- [All-Ireland Pollinator Plan](https://pollinators.ie/), so the surfacing choice protects the habitats it crosses.
- [Leave No Trace Ireland](https://www.leavenotraceireland.org/), accessibility upgrades shouldn't widen impact on the bog itself.
