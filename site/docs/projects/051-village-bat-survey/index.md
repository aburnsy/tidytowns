## Description

A few people around the village have mentioned seeing bats flitting over the Old Road Triangle at dusk in the warmer months, and a couple more have spotted them along the Bog Walk where it runs by the drains. That is genuinely all we know. We don't know which species, we don't know how many, we don't know where they're roosting. Nobody on the committee is a bat expert. Before we do anything bat-related in the village, we want to find out a bit more.

The rough plan for 2028 is three steps. First, ask someone who actually knows what they're doing to come down for an evening and give us a guided bat walk, ideally with a detector so we can start to tell a Pipistrelle from a Leisler's by ear. Second, with whatever we've learned, walk a fixed route through the village a handful of evenings in similar weather and write down what we hear, so that whoever comes after us has something to compare against. Third, only after that, have a conversation about whether bat boxes would actually help anywhere, or whether the village already has plenty of natural roosts in the mature trees and older buildings we have.

We're keen not to get ahead of ourselves. If the survey ends up showing the village is already in good shape for bats, that's a brilliant result on its own. If it points to a gap we could fill, then we'd want to do that properly, with the survey behind us, and have a look again a couple of years later to see if it made any difference.

## What we've read so far

Ireland has nine resident bat species. From [Bat Conservation Ireland](https://www.batconservationireland.org/irish-bats/species), the ones that seem most likely in a village like ours, with mature trees, hedgerows, old stone buildings and a churchyard, are Common Pipistrelle, Soprano Pipistrelle, Leisler's, Brown Long-eared, and possibly Daubenton's anywhere there's still water. We're not in a position to confirm any of that ourselves, which is the whole reason for this project.

For the expert walk, [Bat Conservation Ireland](https://www.batconservationireland.org/what-we-do) is the national body and runs guided walks and training. The [Limerick Bat Group](https://limerickbatgroup.wixsite.com/website) is the nearest active local group we've found. We'll write to both and see what's possible.

For the baseline survey, our reading suggests a fixed transect walk with a basic detector is a sensible starting point for a community group, repeated the same way each year so the numbers are at least comparable. A simple heterodyne detector seems to be in the €100-€150 range, with a recording detector for proper species ID a step up from there. We'll take guidance from BCI on the right kit and the right method before we commit to anything. Bat Conservation Ireland also runs a national [Daubenton's Waterway Survey](https://www.batconservationireland.org/what-we-do/monitoring-distribution-projects/ireland-daubentons-bat-waterways-survey) each August, and the Bog Walk drains might be a candidate stretch to feed into that, which would be a way to get expert eyes on the village without us having to invent a methodology.

For bat boxes, if we get that far, the [BCI Bat Boxes guidance](https://www.batconservationireland.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/BCIrelandGuidelines_BatBoxes.pdf) is the document we'd be following. The bits that have stuck with us are that uptake tends to be slow (often years rather than months), boxes generally go up in clusters facing different directions rather than one on its own, and they work best as a complement to existing natural roosts, not a substitute. We'd lean on the guidance, and on whoever we've engaged for the walk, before deciding any of the practical details.

## Legal context

Bats and their roosts are strictly protected in Ireland under the Wildlife Acts and the European Communities (Birds and Natural Habitats) Regulations 2011. From what we've read, it's an offence to disturb a bat or a roost whether bats are present or not, and any work on a known roost needs a licence from the [National Parks and Wildlife Service](https://www.npws.ie/licences/disturbance/bats-or-otters). The practical version for us as a committee is to keep our distance from any suspected roost, not handle bats, and flag any village building work where a roost might be present to NPWS or BCI before it starts. The [BCI protection page](https://www.batconservationireland.org/irish-bats/protection-law) is where we'd send anyone who wants the detail.

## What success looks like

By the end of 2028 we'd like a short written note for the village covering what we found out, what we heard on the transect evenings, and a rough sense of whether the Old Road Triangle and Bog Walk corridor are doing well by bats or could do better. That note becomes the thing we compare against in future years. If we keep walking the same route the same way, eventually we'll have something honest to say about how bats are getting on in the village, instead of just guessing.
