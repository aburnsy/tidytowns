## Description

Rather than treat the wildflower idea as a single spot, we've gone out to every estate in the village and asked them to consider letting a section grow naturally. Some estates are managed by the residents themselves, others by Tipperary County Council, so it needs both conversations.

The aim is a network of small unmown patches across the village rather than one showpiece, so pollinators have stepping stones from one end to the other, and birds and insects have somewhere to feed and shelter through the winter too.

## Progress (May 2026)

**Leighton Manor have agreed.** The area where we planted alliums last autumn will be left unmown, so the alliums come up through the longer grass and the native seed bank gets a chance.

We've reached out to every other private estate and asked the council Roads section about the estates they manage (since their crew does the mowing in many of them and would need to know which patch to leave). None have said no, but no one else has met up to fully consider it. We're hoping that once the Leighton Manor patch settles in and people see how well it looks, neighbours will follow suit.

The committee has also decided to leave the area surrounding the seating area grow wild this year. The alliums planted there are already coming up alongside dandelion clocks and cow parsley and it looks fantastic. That one is written up under [Unmown verge strips](../../completed/009-unmown-verge-strips/index.md).

## How we'll manage them

Each agreed patch follows the same routine. Leave it unmown from spring through summer so the wildflowers can flower and set seed. Leave the dead stems standing right through winter, so seed-heads feed the birds and the hollow stems give insects somewhere to shelter through the cold months. One cut in early spring before the insects come out, lifting the cut material and any thatch buildup so the wildflowers come up cleanly the following year. Pull dock and thistle through the growing season. Small "managed for wildlife" sign on each so it reads as intentional.

## What the adjudicator said

The 2025 adjudicator suggested the Glen Carraig entrance specifically. We took the principle and widened it to every estate, since pollinators need a network not a single corner.

## Best practice

Following the [All-Ireland Pollinator Plan grasslands guidance](https://pollinators.ie/grasslands/): native seed bank rather than commercial mixes, single annual cut and lift, no fertiliser or seeding.
