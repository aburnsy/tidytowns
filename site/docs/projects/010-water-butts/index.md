## Description

Catching rainwater off village roofs and using it on the village's planters and in-ground beds rather than mains water. The village does not run hanging baskets, so the use case is the trough planters and the beds. Better water for the plants, lower running cost, and a visible sustainability story for the village.

## What the adjudicator said

The 2025 adjudicator asked whether we had considered installing water butts to make watering containers more convenient and more environmentally responsible.

## Where we are (May 2026)

The Sensory Garden has had a large water butt in active use for some years. The watering team draws from it through the season. It is the established backbone of our rainwater capture and the answer to the adjudicator's question is partly that we have been doing this for a while.

To extend coverage further, several volunteers also top up the village's blue trough planters from their own home water butts. **Eileen Corcoran** at Meadow Brook is one of the regulars on that round. Bringing water from home is not ideal long term, but it keeps the planters watered through dry spells while we scout additional village-controlled sites.

The next candidate is the cast-iron downpipe at the corner of St James Church, which would feed a butt at that gable. The church is on the Record of Protected Structures (TRPS840), so any installation needs Heritage Officer sign-off before we know whether it is even possible. That question is in with Róisín O Grady (County Heritage Officer) alongside the forge replica window query. Photo: `church/guttering-may-2026.jpg` on the [village map](../../map.md).

Other potential sites are being scouted; useable spots have to combine a feed-in roof, owner agreement, and somewhere the watering team can actually reach the butt.
