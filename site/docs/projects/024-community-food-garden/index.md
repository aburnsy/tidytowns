## Description

A small food-growing bed in the **sensory garden**, planted out in **September/October 2026** with **onions and garlic**. Both are deliberately chosen as low-maintenance autumn-planted crops that sit happily in the ground over winter, need almost no attention through the summer holidays when the children are out of school, and lift in early summer of the following year.

This connects the school directly to a productive piece of growing without committing the volunteer team to summer-long watering and weeding while the school is closed — the failure mode that kills most school food-growing projects.

## Why this scope works

- **Autumn planting, summer harvest** — onions and garlic are planted in autumn, sit dormant through winter, bulk up in spring while school is back, and lift in May–July. The crop window aligns with the school year rather than fighting it.
- **Low maintenance** — once planted, they need very little water or weeding compared to summer crops like courgettes or salad. A short tidy in early spring is enough.
- **Educational** — children plant in autumn, see the green tops emerge, harvest in early summer. Visible life cycle inside one school year.
- **Builds on the rejuvenated sensory garden** — uses the space already revived by [project 018](../../completed/018-sensory-garden-maintenance/index.md) and the [willow weaving](../../completed/048-sensory-garden-willow-reweaving/index.md).

## Future expansion

If the autumn 2026 bed lands well, the food-growing element can expand in 2027/28 with other low-summer-maintenance crops (rhubarb, perennial herbs) — but every addition must pass the same "doesn't fall over while the school is closed" test.
