# Planting and Species List

What's actually growing across the village. Common names throughout, grouped by where it lives. Pulled from our 2026 photo set, project files and the autumn 2025 bulb planting record.

---

## Spring bulbs (planted autumn 2025, in flower spring 2026)

A single autumn 2025 planting weekend put 11,100 pollinator-friendly bulbs in the ground across the village core, the N75 approach from Thurles, the seating area and several estate entrances. All naturalising varieties chosen for multi-year value and All-Ireland Pollinator Plan compatibility.

| Variety | Quantity | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Ruby Giant crocus | 5,000 | Cluain Na Seimre entrance green; Glen Carraig roadside; mixed estate verges |
| Dutch Master daffodils | 1,600 | N75 western approach from Thurles; Glen Carraig limestone wall; east L4202 verge outside Leighton Manor (about 100) |
| Drumstick alliums | 2,500 | Mixed beds and verges across the village |
| Purple Sensation alliums | 1,000 | Glen Carraig limestone wall; village seating area; Leighton Manor wildflower strip |
| Nigrum alliums | 1,000 | Leighton Manor wildflower strip |

Total cost €1,143.51, split between the committee (around €320) and the participating estates (the rest).

## Planters and structured beds (perennial mix)

The village mix in our locally-made blue planters and main flower beds. Default to perennials we can split between beds rather than annuals we have to buy in fresh every year.

- **Village planter mix:** Geum, ornamental Grass and Achillea around any perennials already in the box that we keep in place rather than rip out.
- **Main monument flower bed:** Poppies, Salvia, Lamb's Ear, Phlox, Wallflowers, Lobelia, Hebe (clipped for structure), Box (clipped for structure).
- **Community Garden sign bed:** Arabis, Heuchera, Hebe.
- **Mixed beds:** Foxgloves, Anemone, Catmint.
- **Meadow Brook planters:** Geum, Achillea, Salvia, Deschampsia (ornamental grass).

## Wildflower areas and unmown verges (no sown seed mix)

We follow All-Ireland Pollinator Plan grassland guidance: let the existing native seed bank come up rather than sowing in a commercial mix, manage by removing dock and thistle from April to September, cut-and-remove in autumn to keep soil nutrients low.

- **St James Church grounds:** existing unmown wildflower bed (no signage and no formal management cycle yet; both would follow if the parish takes up *Returning to Nature*).
- **Leighton Manor roadside strip:** wildflower strip with Allium Purple Sensation and Nigrum, Chamomile and native grasses coming through. The estate is the only one where this is established on the ground.
- **Castle Park boundary:** native seed-bank emergence under mature Sycamore canopy. Dandelion clocks, Cow Parsley and other native wildflowers.

**In conversation, not yet on the ground:**

- **Wildflower patches in every estate:** the 2025 adjudicator flagged the Glen Carraig entrance specifically; the committee took the principle and went out to every estate and the council Roads section asking for a small unmown patch in each (Project 016). Leighton Manor have agreed; others are still considering. Each patch is left standing through winter for seed-heads and stems, then a single early-spring cut.
- **Approach-road inside verges:** committee has proposed unmown strips on the N75, Ballyduff Road and L4202 (Project 009), with the conversation open with the County Biodiversity Officer and council Roads section. The mowing is the council's, not ours, so this is a multi-year council relationship rather than something we can deliver on our own.

## Native trees and structural shrubs (hedgerow and ornamental)

The village's structural backbone. Most are mature; some are recent additions still bedding in.

- **Hawthorn.** The traditional Irish "May tree" and a foundational native, supporting hundreds of insect, bird and small mammal species. At Fanning Park entrance, in the sensory garden, along hedgerows and at the back of the cemetery.
- **Whitebeam.** Cream blossom in May, bright red berries in autumn that the thrushes go mad for. Footpath outside Ardleigh on the L4202.
- **Silver Birch.** Hundreds of insect species feed on it, and the white papery bark is a stand-out feature any time of year. Ned Hayes Park back boundary; opposite Castle Park; a multi-stemmed specimen at the Old Road Triangle.
- **Sycamore.** Mature canopy at Castle Park boundary; along the Black River by Blackcastle.
- **Copper Beech.** The signature avenue inside the wall at Meadow Brook.
- **Hazel.** Multi-stemmed mature coppice at the Old Road Triangle; supports nesting birds, dormice and early-spring catkin pollinators. Also along the Bog Walk.
- **Willow.** Sensory garden (planted living willow gateway and dome), Bog Walk, Black River bank.
- **Alder.** Black River bank, doing the job of stabilising bank and shading the water.
- **Blackthorn.** N75 south corridor, hedgerows.
- **Gorse.** Year-round nectar source for early bumblebees and key shelter for ground-nesting birds. Along the N75 south of the village.
- **Cherry blossom.** Either side of the Sensory Garden.
- **Crab Apple.** Dún Na Rí estate entrance, in deep purple-red foliage.
- **Weigela.** Pink-flowering corner shrub at Cluain Na Seimre.
- **Cypress.** Old Cypress framing the original cemetery gates.
- **Box and Laurel.** Topiary at Ardleigh entrance and the village seating area.

## Bog Walk wildflower and habitat species

The Bog Walk Loop runs about 6.5 km through bogland, wet meadow, hedgerow and regenerating woodland. What we see along the route:

- **Wet bog:** Bog Cotton (in seed June onwards, a strong indicator of healthy raised bog).
- **Wet meadow:** Purple Loosestrife (lights up the damp margins in late summer), Ragwort.
- **Bogland and verge wildflowers:** Knapweed, Hogweed, Yarrow, Field Poppy, Bramble, Dog Rose.
- **Pollinators recorded:** Honeybee on Crocus and Allium, Small Tortoiseshell butterfly on Knapweed, Common Darter dragonfly, Pale Tussock moth caterpillar.
- **Birds and mammals:** Red Squirrel sighted directly on the path (Ireland's only native squirrel, a protected species).

## Old Road Triangle (no-touch policy)

Untouched since the N75 was rerouted in 2010. We do not mow, tidy or lift fallen wood here. Birdlife observed: Wren, Blackcap, Chiffchaff, Robin, Chaffinch, Blue Tit, House Sparrow, Goldcrest, Goldfinch, Blackbird. Hazel coppice and Hawthorn arch over the ground. Spanish Bluebells in early May; Stinging Nettle as a workhorse pollinator host plant for Small Tortoiseshell, Peacock and Red Admiral butterfly caterpillars.
