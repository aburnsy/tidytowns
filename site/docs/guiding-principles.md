# Two Mile Borris TidyTowns: Guiding Principles

How the committee operates: how we choose what to plant where, how we spend money, who we work with, and how we keep records. Reviewed annually with the 3 to 5 year plan.

---

## 1. Planting policy: what goes where

The committee maintains three categories of planted space, each with a different purpose and a different default species choice.

**Raised beds and planters.** High-visibility, high-traffic locations such as the village core and the monument area. Limited soil volume and exposure mean these are the hardest places to maintain natives. Default: a mix of pollinator-friendly perennials chosen from the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan recommended list. Replacement plants chosen for value-for-money over multiple seasons rather than single-year display, splitting existing perennials across beds where we can rather than buying in fresh annuals.

**In-ground beds.** Sensory garden, monument area surrounds, cemetery entrance, named beds at estate entrances. Sufficient soil and root depth to grow natives well. Default: native and near-native pollinator-friendly perennials from the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan list, layered to give continuous flowering from March to October. Seed-heads are left for birds in autumn; cutting back is delayed to early spring.

**Wildflower areas and unmown verges.** St James Church grounds, Leighton Manor estate entrance, Glen Carraig estate entrance, inside strip of approach-road verges. Default: no sown seed mix at all. We let the existing native seed bank emerge, manage by removing dock and thistle periodically from April to September, and cut-and-remove in autumn to keep soil nutrients low. Longer term we would like to add "Managed for wildlife" signage so the intent is obvious. This follows All-Ireland Pollinator Plan grassland guidance.

**Species choice criteria, in order of priority:**

1. **Native first.** The default for any new planting is a native species suited to the soil and aspect.
2. **Perennial preferred.** Perennials give multi-year value for the same cash spend; annuals are reserved for high-impact, high-visibility locations.
3. **Pollinator-friendly.** Where neither native nor perennial is practical, the next test is whether the species appears on the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan recommended list.
4. **Simple flower form.** Single-flowered cultivars over double-flowered, so short-tongued bees can access them.
5. **Continuous flowering.** Aim for at least one species in flower every month from March to October across the village's planted areas.
6. **Value for money.** Where two species meet the criteria, the cheaper one wins.

## 2. Cost discipline

- Own-funds budget cap: around €600 per year unless an external grant is secured.
- Any single project costing more than €200 needs a funding source identified before the committee commits.
- Grant-funded projects are tracked separately and do not consume own-funds budget.
- Local nurseries and hardware shops are preferred where price is comparable.
- "Defer don't drop". Ambitious projects we can't afford stay on the register as grant-conditional rather than being abandoned.

---

## 3. Partnerships first

We borrow expertise rather than try to build it ourselves. Our standing partners:

- **Tipperary County Council Heritage Officer, Róisín O Grady.** Heritage building advice and funding routes (forge replica window, heritage signage, Built Heritage Investment Scheme applications). Contacted directly for technical heritage questions.
- **Tipperary County Council Biodiversity Officer, Ann-Marie Fleming.** Species selection and management advice for the graveyard scheme and any biodiversity-rich planting.
- **Tipperary County Council Roads, Thurles District.** Operational channel for verge mowing, approach-road biodiversity, and the M8-exit roundabout.
- **Parish Pastoral Council, St James Church.** Conversation partner on the *Returning to Nature* programme covering the church grounds and cemetery; the parish makes the call on whether and when to formally join.
- **Two Mile Borris National School.** Green Schools partner, with teacher Joann Cussen leading the school's Green Flag application; the school's biodiversity work is part of our village biodiversity story.
- **All-Ireland Pollinator Plan / Pollinators.ie.** Species lists, grassland management guidance, monitoring methodology. The default reference for any planting question.
- **Faith Community Pollinator Plan.** The operational management framework we would use for the church-grounds 30% area if the parish takes up *Returning to Nature*.

Partners are named explicitly in the application narrative.

---

## 4. Documentation

- Photograph year-round across spring, summer, autumn and winter.
- Log clean-up dates, planting decisions and species used, FIT count results when we have them, and any fly-tipping reports at the time.
- The committee maintains this public project tracker so residents and adjudicators can see what we are doing, what is planned, and at what stage. The tracker itself is our 3 to 5 year plan.

---

## 5. Adjudicator recommendations

- Address every recommendation every year, even if the action is only an email or a piece of signage.
- No recommendation is ignored two years running. A recommendation that carries over twice is treated as urgent.
- Action taken is recorded explicitly in the next application ("In response to the 2025 adjudicator's recommendation, we did X").

---

## 6. Review

Reviewed annually at the post-results committee meeting (typically September after July adjudication), alongside the 3 to 5 year plan and the project register. Where a principle produces a result the committee thinks is wrong, the principle is updated rather than worked around.
