The Monument Area is the heart of the village, and the centrepiece is something very few villages in Ireland can match: our All-Ireland Victory Centennial monument. It marks 100 years since Two Mile Borris represented Tipperary and won the 1900 All-Ireland Hurling Final at Jones' Road in Dublin (later renamed Croke Park), where we beat London 2-5 to 0-6. The names of the winning panel are all listed on the centre plaque, with the club's red-and-yellow flying alongside the Tipperary blue-and-gold. The smaller plaque on the cobbles at the foot of the monument records the unveiling on 26th August 2000 by Sean McCague, then President of the GAA, blessed by Most Rev. Dermot Clifford, Archbishop of Cashel and Emly, and erected by Jerome Lennon for Stone Developments Ltd, Carlow.

Around the monument is the main flower bed at the village centre, packed with seasonal colour. In May you have Red Poppies, pink Phlox, yellow Wallflowers, purple Salvia, blue Lobelia and Hebe shrubs all going at once, and we keep year-round structure with clipped Box and the same shrubs that anchor the rest of the village planters. Volunteers split perennials from this bed and use them in our other planters, so a planting day at the monument tends to ripple out around the village. Just up from the bed you have the Community Garden sign, the sensory garden signpost, and the spot we use for the village Christmas tree.

We also have our Mutt Mitt dispenser at the monument, which the 2025 adjudicator found empty when they called in. We have kept it on the volunteer round since, and Cllr Sean Ryan has offered to pull a stockpile down from Clonmel for us. The bollards around the monument are flagged for a repaint as part of an ongoing committee project, again following an adjudicator note.

In December the whole spot turns into the centre of the village calendar. Our Christmas lights switch-on parade comes down the main street with decorated tractors rolling under the lit-up canopy, the singers and the pipers turning out, mulled wine on the go, and Santa coming in on the lead tractor. The whole village gets behind it, and the monument is where it all converges.
