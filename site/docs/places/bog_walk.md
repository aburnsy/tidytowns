The Bog Walk is a 6.5km loop out the back of the village. You head off from the Clover entrance on the main street, walk north through open bog, and come back round by Clover Lane off the Ballyduff Road. It's one of the real gems of the parish. Ten minutes from your front door and you're out in proper wild countryside, and it's also the base for one of our biggest fundraisers of the year.

For habitat you've got the lot. Raised bog still holding water in the lowest patches, dry heath where the heather takes over and gives the whole place a purple hue come August, wet meadow either side of the path, and regenerating woodland in the corners. Sphagnum moss is the engine of the bog, the spongy stuff underfoot that builds the peat in the first place, and the colour of it shifts from green through orange to red depending on the species and how wet the ground is. Heather is the other dead giveaway, with Ling, Bell Heather and Cross-leaved Heath all in there once you start looking. Bog Cotton flares up white through May and June and is a great sign the wet ground is doing what it should. On the drier banks you'll find Tormentil and Bog Asphodel, and if you crouch down at the right spot in the right week, Round-leaved Sundew, the mad little carnivorous plant that catches midges on its sticky red hairs.

Plenty of birdlife along the route. Skylark, Meadow Pipit, Stonechat and Snipe have all been sighted on the open bog. Through late summer and autumn there are big flocks of Goldfinches and Linnets feasting on the Knapweed and Thistle seed heads, which is one of the reasons we leave that stuff stand rather than tidying it away. We've also a pair of Common Buzzard nesting close by, often seen riding the thermals over the loop.

Down at hedge level along the wet meadow you've got Knapweed, Hogweed, Yarrow, Purple Loosestrife and Ragwort doing serious work for the pollinators, with Wild Dog Rose and Bramble flowering away on the post-and-rail fence. Small Tortoiseshell butterflies feed on the Knapweed in summer and we caught a brilliant Pale Tussock moth caterpillar back in September. Common Darter dragonflies bask on the fence rails right through autumn, and 2024 was something else entirely, the place was absolutely littered with them, a proper sight to behold. Frogs croak from the wetter patches in spring, and there's a fair chance of an Irish Hare bolting across the path if you're out early enough.

The headline though is the Red Squirrels. Ireland's only native squirrel, fully protected, and a sure sign the woodland and hedge corridors around the loop are in decent shape. We've had a few good photos of them on the route now. You wouldn't get them where the habitat was wrong.

The loop is in constant use. There's a regular crew out on it every morning come hail, rain or shine, and various village groups run charity walks along the route through the year. St Stephen's Day always brings out a walk or two for different village causes, including Tidy Towns ourselves, and the Lions Club run a few of their own. The annual St Bridget's Day Clover Bog Walk is the big fundraising one for us. The 2025 outing pulled in €1000 for the cemetery development, which went straight into the new extension.
