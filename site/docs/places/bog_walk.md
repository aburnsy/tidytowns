The Bog Walk is a looped walk of about 6.5km out the back of the village, starting from the Clover entrance off the main street, heading north through open bog, and returning via Clover Lane off the Ballyduff Road. It has become one of the real fixtures of the village, both as a bit of countryside on our doorstep and as the base for one of our biggest annual community events.

The habitat along the route is a beautiful mix of raised bog, wet meadow, dry heath and regenerating woodland. Bog Cotton is the classic sight on the wet bog, with its white fluffy seedheads through the early summer, and it is a great indicator that the wet ground is in good condition. Through the season you get Knapweed, Hogweed, Yarrow, Purple Loosestrife and Ragwort filling out the meadows alongside the path, and Wild Dog Rose and Bramble flowering on the post-and-rail fence. All of those species do an enormous amount of work for our pollinators and put on a fantastic show into the bargain.

The wildlife along the route says everything about the habitat quality. We have seen and photographed Small Tortoiseshell butterflies on the Knapweed, Common Darter dragonflies basking on the fence rails through the late summer and autumn, and a brilliant Pale Tussock moth caterpillar in September. The real treat is our Red Squirrel sightings, Ireland's only native squirrel and a protected species, which is a brilliant sign of quality woodland and hedgerow habitat all the way around the loop.

The walk also feeds straight back into village projects through the annual St Bridget's Day Clover Bog Walk fundraiser. The 2025 walk brought in €1000 for the village graveyard development, which we put to work on the new cemetery extension. It is a brilliant fixture in the calendar, gets the whole community out, and turns one of the loveliest bits of countryside in the parish into something that supports the rest of what we do.
