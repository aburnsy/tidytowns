---
title: "Bronze Age cremation cemetery (AR 32)"
marker_id: dig_ar32
category: "Heritage"
---

A small but very tightly packed Middle Bronze Age flat cremation cemetery, dug 350 metres southwest of the village in August and September 2006. The features all sat within an area 12 metres north to south by 15 metres east to west, on the brow of a low ridge in what is now tillage ground.

The excavation team identified nine cremation pits, with three further pits classed as uncertain burials, plus another eleven shallow "blind" cremation or cenotaph pits. The remarkable thing about the layout is that, except for one case, none of the pits cut into each other. That tells you the cemetery was clearly marked above ground, probably with stakes, small stones or wooden posts. The upper fill of one of the pits actually held a posthole, which seals it. Three radiocarbon dates were returned from three of the cremation pits: 1619-1454 cal BC, 1603-1427 cal BC, and 1492-1322 cal BC. Multiple post-medieval plough furrows running north to south truncated many of the features, which is how a lot of them came to be only partially preserved by the time the diggers reached them.

This is the cemetery the early medieval people up the road at the AR 33 ringfort would have known about as the "old people's resting place". The 3,500 year-old graves were already prehistoric to them. For more context see [Prehistoric Two Mile Borris](../heritage/prehistoric.md). The full report is on the [Digital Repository of Ireland](https://repository.dri.ie/catalog/8049vk941).
