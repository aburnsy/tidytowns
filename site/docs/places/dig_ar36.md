About a kilometre southeast of AR 33, on the eastern slope of a low hill overlooking the Black River and the western edge of Littleton Bog, AR 36 produced one of the more striking combinations on the M8 scheme: an Early Bronze Age cremation cemetery and a separate phase of early medieval iron-smithing in the same field, more than three thousand years apart.

The cemetery came first. Thirteen pits contained the burnt remains of thirteen individuals, with a further five pits interpreted as cenotaphs or "blind" burials. Two radiocarbon dates put the burials between 2204-2038 and 2024-1896 cal BC, an Early Bronze Age range broadly similar in time to the trough underneath the AR 35 burnt mound, about seven hundred metres up the road.

Then nothing for nearly three thousand years, until the early medieval period rolled back across the ridge. A large V-shaped linear ditch dated to 639-771 cal AD was cut across the area, and a conjoined curvilinear enclosure was added in the 9th to 10th century (dated 894-991 cal AD). Beside the ditch and inside the enclosure, the diggers found a number of charcoal- and slag-rich "bloom-smithing" hearths, where wrought iron blooms were being worked into usable tools. Four medieval-period metal artefacts came out of the ditch fills, including two whittle-tang knives and what may be the blade of a sword or a dagger.

The marker location on this map is provisional: the Irish National Grid easting given in the published report is impossible (it places the site on the Wexford coast, nowhere near the M8). We have temporarily placed AR 36 between [AR 35](dig_ar35.md) and [AR 37](dig_ar37.md), since the AR 32 report describes both AR 36 and AR 37 as "one kilometre east" of AR 32. We've asked TII to confirm the true coordinate. The shape and content of the site is well-recorded though, even if its exact position needs to be pinned down.

For more on the Early Bronze Age and the early medieval activity in the parish, see [Prehistoric Two Mile Borris](../heritage/prehistoric.md) and [Early Medieval Two Mile Borris](../heritage/early-medieval.md).
