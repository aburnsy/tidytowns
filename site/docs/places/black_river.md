The Black River runs along the western edge of the village, slipping past the foot of Blackcastle and ducking under the road on its way south. It is a tributary of the River Drish, which feeds into the wider Suir catchment, so what flows through here is part of the bigger water story for our part of Tipperary.

The crossing itself is a quiet little spot. Sycamore arches over the water in summer giving the whole stretch a green tunnel feel, and the banks south of the village carry mature Willow and Alder, two of the most valuable native trees you can have along a riverbank. They stabilise the bank, shade the water and provide cover for fish and the odd dipper. We see Coots and ducks on the river regularly, and the corridor of vegetation along the banks links the village neatly into the wider countryside.
