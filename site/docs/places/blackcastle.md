The Blackcastle is the four-storey Norman tower house that stands at the western edge of the village, on the approach in from the N75. It is a fine big solid tower house with bartizans, the defensive stone projections at the corners, and even with the wear of centuries on the stonework it has lost very little of its presence. It probably dates from the 16th century, which makes it a late-medieval Anglo-Norman structure rather than a survival of the original Norman invasion period.

The wider story is older though, and very much tied up with the village name. "Borris" comes from the Norman word for a borough or stronghold, and "Two-Mile" refers to the distance from Thurles, two Irish miles being about 4km. Right beside the tower house is a ringwork site, the earthwork remains of an earlier fortification, which suggests there was a defended settlement on this corner of the parish well before the tower we see today was built. The tower house, the ringwork and a medieval church and graveyard site in the same cluster of townlands are all listed together in the national monuments record.

The keep itself is now incorporated into a working farmyard, with a lovely red-trim cottage out front that gives you a real sense of just how big the tower really is. Around the back, across an unmown meadow, you get one of the best views in the village in May, when the Hawthorn comes into full blossom and you have five hundred years of Norman stonework, a season of native white blossom and a meadow full of pollinator habitat all in the one frame. Hawthorn is the traditional Irish May tree and a foundational native species for our biodiversity plan, so the meadow at the back of the castle is doing real work for the village even before you get to the heritage of the building itself.

Most of the medieval features uncovered during the 2007 to 2009 M8 archaeological excavation were found in fields just south of here, in Borris and Blackcastle townlands. For the wider medieval story, see [Medieval Two Mile Borris](../heritage/medieval.md) in our Heritage section.
