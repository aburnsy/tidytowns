Scoil Mochaomhóg Naofa NS is the village national school, sitting on the L4202 right beside the Sensory Garden, and there is a serious amount of work going on inside the gates that ties straight into the wider Tidy Towns effort. The school is currently working towards its 8th Green Flag, this one for Global Citizenship Marine. Past flags don't go on the shelf once they are won either. Each previous theme stays alive year on year, which means there is a constant stream of Green Schools work coming out of the school community.

The school opened on 13th March 1846, the feast day of [St Mochoemog](liathmore.md) who founded the monastery at Liathmore. The first school was a small slated rectangle on the other side of the road, built on a plot leased from Count John O'Reilly of Delvin with a £60 government grant. That building closed in 1912 when the present school was completed, and the cast 1846 plaque on the front wall of the current building marks the founding date rather than the building itself. Either way, it is an unbroken line of community education in the village for nearly 180 years.

National Spring Clean has been part of school life for the last 8 years, with classes heading out around different parts of the village to do the litter pick. This year the school also signed up to Clean Up Tipp in Term 1, and after a brilliant turnout and great enthusiasm from the kids it was decided to run a litter pick every term from now on. The Picker Pals programme has children taking home a litter pick kit on a rota and getting stuck in around their own local areas at home, which spreads the effort right out into corners we wouldn't otherwise reach. A recent battery recycling drive saw collection boxes placed in local premises around the village, and the response from the community was great.

The school grounds themselves are kept beautifully, with the planters in the front yard well tended and a school garden where the children plant, mind and harvest different fruits and vegetables in raised beds. Active travel is pushed hard, with cycling, scooting and walking to school all encouraged, and the bike shed from An Taisce has made a real difference.
