The cemetery sits at the western end of the village on the L4202, with the Blackcastle visible just beyond on the right. The original gates are a lovely piece of work in their own right, with carved stone urn finials and old wrought iron, framed by an old Cypress that has been standing watch on the right-hand side for a long time. Headstone inscriptions from the old graveyard have been transcribed and donated to Tipperary Studies for the record, so the older end of the burial ground is well documented for anyone tracing a family line through Two-Mile-Borris.

There are two halves to the cemetery now, the old burial ground and the new extension. The new entrance leads in off the L4202, with cut stone pillars, a dedication plaque, a gravel sweep, and a clear set of gates looking through into the new burial ground beyond. Work on the wider extension is still on the go, and it is a real credit to everyone who has put the time in.

At the parking layby in front of the original gates we have two of our blue trough planters either side of the spot, both locally made by a local tradesman as part of the cohesive village-wide planter scheme we are rolling out. The 2025 adjudicator flagged the cemetery as a candidate for a biodiversity-rich landscaping scheme, and we have a project on the books to scope that out with the local biodiversity officer at Tipperary County Council.

The cemetery is also the direct beneficiary of our annual St Bridget's Day Clover Bog Walk fundraiser. The 2025 walk brought in €1000 specifically toward the graveyard development, which is a brilliant example of how the village's wider community life feeds back into the cemetery as a place we all look after.
