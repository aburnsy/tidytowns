There is a wedge of mature trees and rough ground at the western edge of the village that, if you did not know the story, looks like nothing in particular. It used to be the through-road. The N75 ran straight through here for centuries, and then in 2010 the M8 motorway opened at Junction 5 and the N75 was rerouted around the village. Overnight this strip became an island between the new junction and the village edge, and nobody has touched it since.

We made a deliberate decision to leave it alone. No mowing, no tidying, no lifting of fallen wood. Quietly, with no help from anyone, it has turned into a proper little wildlife refuge inside the village edge. Hazel and Hawthorn arch right over the lane and turn the whole space into a green tunnel. There is a beautiful multi-stemmed Hazel coppice in there that took decades to build and would take seconds to destroy, which is the single best argument for the no-touch policy on this piece of ground.

The bird list says everything about the habitat quality. In the summer months you can hear Wren, Blackcap, Chiffchaff, Robin, Chaffinch, Blue Tit, House Sparrow, Goldcrest, Goldfinch and Blackbird from the lane, with the summer-migrant warblers and Ireland's smallest bird (the Goldcrest) all settled in. On the ground in May you get Spanish Bluebells flowering alongside Stinging Nettles. The Spanish Bluebell is the upright one with bells coming off all sides of the stem, easy to tell from the one-sided drooping curve of the native bluebell. The Nettle is a workhorse pollinator host plant, feeding the caterpillars of Small Tortoiseshell, Peacock and Red Admiral butterflies through the summer.

It is honest, untouched ground doing exactly what we want it to do, and it costs us nothing.
