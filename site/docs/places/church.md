St James Church sits across from Tully's on the main street, the calm centrepiece in the heart of the village. The bell-cote stands proud above the three lancet windows on the front gable, and the building is well kept with the parish keeping a close eye on the fabric. Father Tom organised a big deep clean of the whole church back in late 2023, with a Genie cherry-picker reaching right up to the bell-cote and gable, and we were delighted to chip in. You can still see the contrast in the masonry between the freshly cleaned section and the higher reaches.

In the church grounds we have one of our bug hotels, mounted on a recycled tree stump and packed with pine cones, bamboo, drilled timber and reeds. It is five-star accommodation for solitary bees, ladybirds and lacewings, and it is one of the small biodiversity features the school children get a kick out of when they pass.

There is a conversation underway with Father Tom about feeding the cast-iron downpipe at the corner of the church into a water butt, which would give us harvested rainwater for the village containers and baskets and answer a 2025 adjudicator suggestion at the same time. The 2025 adjudicator also flagged the church grounds as a candidate for a wildflower bed to lift the species diversity on site, and we are working through where the right spot for that would sit.
