A second Bronze Age burnt mound site close to AR 35, excavated by Gary Conboy in October 2006 ahead of the M8 build. The site produced three feature groups across the field.

A burnt mound, "fulacht fia" in Irish, is one of the most common prehistoric site types in the Irish landscape. A sunken wooden trough, a fireplace beside it to heat large stones red-hot, and a low horseshoe-shaped mound of cracked, fire-shattered stones that builds up over years of use. What people were doing in the troughs is still debated: cooking, brewing, washing wool, dyeing, and ritual bathing are all candidates.

The earliest activity at AR 37 was to the north (Group 1): an unaccompanied Middle Bronze Age trough dated to 1740-1520 cal BC. The main burnt mound (Site A) sat about thirty metres further south; a trough excavated from underneath it dated to 1367-1129 cal BC. Two more isolated pit clusters complete the picture, plus several later post-medieval linear ditches and field boundaries cut across the prehistoric remains.

The neighbours along the same stretch of the M8 alignment include [AR 35](dig_ar35.md), an older but otherwise similar burnt mound complex, and [AR 36](dig_ar36.md), where the cremation dead were buried in the Early Bronze Age and iron was being smelted nearly three thousand years later. The map position for AR 37 is our conversion of the published Irish National Grid reference and may need a small adjustment when we confirm the coordinates with TII. For wider context see [Prehistoric Two Mile Borris](../heritage/prehistoric.md). The full report is on the [Digital Repository of Ireland](https://repository.dri.ie/catalog/8k721x98k).
