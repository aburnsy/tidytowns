## Description

The village already has a Norman castle, an old forge, a working church and the bog walk entrance, but they sit as four separate stops a visitor has to know about already. A heritage trail is the connective layer: one orientation point in the centre of the village, low-profile wayfinding posts along the route, and a printable / downloadable trail guide so a visitor can step out of the car and walk a logical loop between them.

The **content** at each site (the panels telling the story of the castle, forge, church, bog walk entrance) sits under [project 020 - heritage interpretation panels](../020-heritage-interpretation-panels/index.md). This project is just the connective tissue. The two are designed to be delivered together but priced and scoped separately so either can move first if funding lands for one before the other.

## What it involves

- **One central orientation panel** at the monument or village green. The trail map, a short paragraph on each site, a "you are here", a QR code to the trail page on this website. Post-mounted, weatherproof, in keeping with the rural village look.
- **6 to 8 wayfinding markers** at decision points along the route between the four sites. Small post-style markers, low-profile, just enough to confirm "trail this way" without cluttering the streetscape.
- **A trail page on this website** with a longer write-up of each site, a route map, downloadable PDF leaflet. Mostly volunteer time, and the website is already in place so there's no separate hosting cost.
- **A short print run of A4 leaflets** for Tully's, the two pubs and the church porch, so people who land in the village without their phone can still pick up the route.

## Cost breakdown

- Central orientation panel (research, illustration, fabrication, post, install): €1200 to €2000
- 6 to 8 wayfinding markers at €150 to €250 each: €900 to €2000
- Design and illustration if not done by a volunteer: €0 to €400
- Leaflet print run (250 copies): €80 to €200
- **Total: €2500 to €4500**

That sits a touch below the bog walk Phase 3 panels because the trail is a smaller number of larger items rather than a series of detailed species-ID boards.
